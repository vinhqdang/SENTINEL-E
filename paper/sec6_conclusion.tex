\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Clip-level evaluation hides a statistical problem. A per-frame rule fed valid p-values raises a false alarm on \ValidityPfaPvaluePct\% of 40-minute null streams. This paper makes three contributions.

The theory is an episodic e-process. It mixes over episodes that start, end and recur. It is a non-negative supermartingale, so Ville's inequality gives time-uniform false-alarm control, and it contains the changepoint mixture as the case in which episodes never end.

The algorithm is a two-state forward recursion. It runs in constant time per frame, at \CostSentinelUs\,\textmu s per frame, with the p-value and the e-detector step computed once per betting lag.

The evaluation reports realised rather than nominal false-alarm rates, censored delays and paired comparisons on a shared betting grid.

The simulated results are the strongest. Week-long null streams keep the false-alarm probability at 0.000, while per-frame thresholds fail within an hour. The episodic mixture shortens delay for long, intermittent and recurring events, and it does not for single or permanent changes.

The real-video results are more limited. On UCSD Ped2, CUHK Avenue and Mivia-IWDD, the per-video false-alarm rate stays within sampling error on the two single-camera corpora. Power depends on the backbone and remains limited. Optical flow detects 11 of 12 Ped2 episodes but 3 of 74 Avenue episodes, and PatchCore detects 10 of 50 Mivia-IWDD dumping videos. On Mivia-IWDD, where calibration pools training videos, PatchCore raises alarms on 9 of 50 normal videos at $\alpha=0.1$, above the nominal level. The guarantee needs calibration from the monitored camera, and the pooled Mivia-IWDD setting does not meet that condition.

Three directions follow. The first is a stronger, dumping-specific backbone, which would raise power without changing the theory. The second is per-camera calibration on Mivia-IWDD, which would test the guarantee under the condition it needs. The third is longer real streams, which would test the week-long simulation directly. Residue-specific change models would also separate the dumping action from the waste left behind. The argument and the algorithm do not depend on dumping, so the same optional-stopping problem applies to wildlife poaching, illegal fishing and infrastructure faults.
