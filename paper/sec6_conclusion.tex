\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Continuous monitoring needs its own evaluation. A per-frame rule fed valid p-values raises a false alarm on \ValidityPfaPvaluePct\% of 40-minute null streams, so a clip-level level does not carry over to a stream. This paper makes three contributions.

The theory is an episodic e-process. It mixes over episodes that start, end and recur. It is a non-negative supermartingale when the p-values are conditionally super-uniform, so Ville's inequality gives time-uniform false-alarm control, and it contains the changepoint mixture as the case in which episodes never end. On real video the conditional premise is not met after thinning, so validity there is verified empirically (\cref{sec:limitations}).

The algorithm is a two-state forward recursion. It runs in constant time per frame, with the p-value and the e-detector step computed once per betting lag. The guarantee layer costs \CostSentinelUs\,\textmu s per frame in batch and about 2\,\textmu s per frame when streaming (1.98\,\textmu s predicted).

The evaluation reports realised rather than nominal false-alarm rates, censored delays and paired comparisons on grids of equal size.

The simulated results cover horizons of up to one week. No false alarm occurred in 200 week-long null streams (95\% upper limit 0.019, which does not by itself resolve $\alpha=0.01$), and per-frame thresholds reach 0.980 within an hour. The episodic mixture shortens delay for long, intermittent and recurring events. For a single change the changepoint mixture is faster, and for a permanent change the two are equivalent.

The real-video results are more limited in scale. On UCSD Ped2, CUHK Avenue and Mivia-IWDD, the per-video false-alarm proportions are consistent with the nominal level at $\alpha=0.1$ on all three corpora. The null footage is short (12.6\,s and 6.9\,min for Ped2 and Avenue). On Mivia-IWDD, PatchCore raises alarms on 9 of 50 normal videos, with a 95\% interval of [0.086, 0.314]. Alarm rates per hour of null footage are large, at 100.5 per hour for PatchCore on Mivia-IWDD. Power depends on the backbone. Optical flow detects 11 of 12 Ped2 episodes and 3 of 74 Avenue episodes, and PatchCore detects 10 of 50 Mivia-IWDD dumping videos. Neither backbone is state of the art. On these short videos, several classical baselines selected by the same training-only rule detect comparable numbers of episodes, and no advantage for SENTINEL-E is claimed. Its advantage is the horizon-free guarantee, which the short videos cannot test.

Three directions follow. The first is a stronger, dumping-specific backbone, which would raise power without changing the theory. The second is per-camera calibration on Mivia-IWDD, which would test the guarantee under the condition it needs and show whether pooling accounts for the Mivia-IWDD proportion. The third is longer real streams, which would test the week-long simulation directly. Residue-specific change models would also separate the dumping action from the waste left behind. The argument and the algorithm do not depend on dumping, so the same optional-stopping problem applies to wildlife poaching, illegal fishing and infrastructure faults.
