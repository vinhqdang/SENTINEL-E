\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Clip-level evaluation hides a statistical problem. A per-frame rule fed valid p-values raises a false alarm on \ValidityPfaPvaluePct\% of 40-minute null streams. This paper makes three contributions.

The theory is an episodic e-process. It mixes over episodes that start, end and recur. It is a non-negative supermartingale when the p-values are conditionally super-uniform, so Ville's inequality gives time-uniform false-alarm control. It contains the changepoint mixture as the case in which episodes never end. Real video does not meet the conditional premise (\cref{sec:limitations}), so the guarantee there is an empirical property, not a theorem.

The algorithm is a two-state forward recursion. It runs in constant time per frame, with the p-value and the e-detector step computed once per betting lag. The guarantee layer costs \CostSentinelUs\,\textmu s per frame in batch and about 2\,\textmu s per frame when streaming (1.98\,\textmu s predicted).

The evaluation reports realised rather than nominal false-alarm rates, censored delays and paired comparisons on grids of equal size.

The simulated results are the strongest. No false alarm occurred in 200 week-long null streams (95\% upper limit 0.019, which does not by itself resolve $\alpha=0.01$), while per-frame thresholds fail within an hour. The episodic mixture shortens delay for long, intermittent and recurring events. For a single change it is behind, and for a permanent change the two are equivalent.

The real-video results are more limited. On UCSD Ped2, CUHK Avenue and Mivia-IWDD, the per-video false-alarm rate is consistent with the nominal level on the two single-camera corpora, but their null footage is short (12.6\,s and 6.9\,min). On Mivia-IWDD, PatchCore raises alarms on 9 of 50 normal videos at $\alpha=0.1$, a point estimate above the nominal level whose interval contains it. The cause is not isolated: pooled calibration, serial dependence and boundary selection of the lag are all candidates. Alarm rates per hour of null footage are large, at 100.5 per hour for PatchCore on Mivia-IWDD. Power depends on the backbone and remains limited. Optical flow detects 11 of 12 Ped2 episodes but 3 of 74 Avenue episodes, and PatchCore detects 10 of 50 Mivia-IWDD dumping videos. Neither backbone is state of the art. On real video SENTINEL-E shows no demonstrated advantage over the baselines. Its claimed advantage, the horizon-free guarantee, is one that the short real videos cannot test.

Three directions follow. The first is a stronger, dumping-specific backbone, which would raise power without changing the theory. The second is per-camera calibration on Mivia-IWDD, which would test the guarantee under the condition it needs and show whether pooling explains the excess. The third is longer real streams, which would test the week-long simulation directly. Residue-specific change models would also separate the dumping action from the waste left behind. The argument and the algorithm do not depend on dumping, so the same optional-stopping problem applies to wildlife poaching, illegal fishing and infrastructure faults.
