\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

A camera produces frames $x_1, x_2, \ldots$ and a frozen detector
$g$ produces scores $s_t = g(x_t) \in \mathbb{R}$, with higher meaning more
dumping-like. Alongside each frame we observe a low-dimensional context vector
$c_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ --- time of day, a weather flag, ambient luminance, a
background-subtraction motion measure --- available to any deployed camera
without privileged knowledge. The operator supplies a per-camera calibration set
$\cD = \{(s_i^{\mathrm{cal}}, c_i^{\mathrm{cal}})\}_{i=1}^{n}$ of confirmed
no-dumping frames.

Write $\Pinf$ for the law of the stream under ``no dumping ever'' and
$\mathbb{P}_{\nu}$ for the law when dumping begins at frame $\nu$. A detection
rule is a stopping time $\tau$ with respect to the observation filtration
$(\Fcal_t)$. We require:

\begin{definition}[Time-uniform false-alarm control]
\label{def:tuv}
A rule has time-uniform false-alarm level $\alpha$ if
$\Pinf(\tau < \infty) \le \alpha$.
\end{definition}

The quantifier is the whole point. A per-frame guarantee
$\Pinf(\text{alarm at } t) \le \alpha$ for each fixed $t$ says nothing about
$\Pinf(\exists t : \text{alarm at } t)$, and the gap between them grows with the
horizon. \Cref{def:tuv} is what a municipality is implicitly buying when it is
told a detector has a $1\%$ false-alarm rate.

Dumping is episodic, so we write $\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{E}}$ for the law when a
set of episodes $\mathcal{E} = \{[\nu_1, \nu_1 + L_1], \ldots\}$ occurs, within
each of which a fraction $\pi$ of frames are anomalous. The single-persistent-change
model is the special case of one episode with $L = \infty$ and $\pi = 1$.

We report, over Monte-Carlo replicates:
$\PFA$, the probability that a null stream of a stated length raises at least
one alarm; $\ARL$, the mean frames to a false alarm on a null stream, censored
at the horizon; $\ADD$, the mean frames from the first onset $\nu_1$ to the
first alarm at or after it, over runs that alarm; and the miss rate, the
fraction of post-change segments ending unflagged.

\paragraph{A measurement pitfall, and the fix.}
Conditional $\ADD$ is averaged over a \emph{different subset of runs for each
method}: a detector that misses the hard events is scored only on the easy ones
and therefore looks fast. Since the methods compared here differ substantially
in miss rate, we also report a \emph{censored} delay in which a run that never
alarms is charged the whole remaining horizon rather than dropped. The two can
rank methods oppositely --- in \cref{sec:episodes} the changepoint mixture has
the shorter conditional delay and the longer censored delay --- and where they
disagree the censored figure is the comparable one, so head-to-head claims in
this paper rest on it. This is not specific to our setting and we suspect it
affects published comparisons elsewhere.

Every comparison is made at a method's \emph{realised} $\PFA$, never its nominal
one, since a method whose nominal level is meaningless would otherwise appear to
dominate by misreporting its own error rate.

\paragraph{From clips to streams.}
A clip-labelled corpus such as Mivia-IWDD becomes a streaming benchmark without
new data collection. Reserve a disjoint subset of confirmed-negative clips as
$\cD$; concatenate the remaining negative clips into a background stream of the
desired length; splice positive clips in at random onsets. Holding the
calibration clips out preserves the exchangeability the conformal layer needs.
This protocol is implemented in the library
(\texttt{build\_stream\_from\_clips}) and is how \cref{sec:real-data}'s Ped2
and Avenue streams are built once per-frame backbone scores for a real corpus
are in hand.

\subsection{Layer 1: conformal calibration}
\label{sec:layer1}

\paragraph{Betting rate.}
The e-process of \cref{sec:episodic} requires increments that are conditionally
super-uniform given the past. Video violates this badly: at $25$\,fps the
lag-one autocorrelation of backbone scores exceeds $0.85$ in our streams. We
estimate a decorrelation lag $\ell$ from the calibration set alone --- projecting
out the context-predictable drift first with a quadratic least-squares fit, since
otherwise the slow diurnal component dominates the autocorrelation and returns a
uselessly large $\ell$ --- and place one bet per $\ell$ frames. In our streams
$\ell = \AblLag$, so bets occur more than once per second, far finer than the
timescale of a dumping event. The same reasoning applies to the calibration
frames, which are consecutive video too: they are thinned by $\ell$ before use,
so the honest calibration size is $n/\ell$ and it is that number the guarantee is
stated in. \Cref{sec:ablation} shows this is not a refinement: betting on every
frame is the single largest source of invalidity we measured.

\paragraph{Conditional calibration.}
Let $S$ be a nonconformity score. The split-conformal p-value of a deployment
frame is $p = (1 + |\{i : S_i \ge S\}|)/(n+1)$, which is super-uniform under
exchangeability. Exchangeability between a night-time frame and a
daylight-dominated $\cD$ is exactly what fails. We therefore conformalise the
\emph{context-normalised residual}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residual}
  z \;=\; \frac{s - \hat g(c)}{\hat\sigma(c)},
  \qquad
  \hat g(c) \approx \mathbb{E}[s \mid c],
  \quad
  \hat\sigma(c) \approx \mathbb{E}\bigl[|s - \hat g(c)| \,\big|\, c\bigr],
\end{equation}
with $\hat g$ and $\hat\sigma$ ridge regressions on a quadratic-with-interactions
basis in $c$, fitted on one half of $\cD$ and conformalised on the other. The
split matters: it makes the conformalisation residuals independent of the fitted
models, so conditionally on those models \cref{thm:beta} applies verbatim. What
remains assumed is that the normalised residual in \cref{eq:residual} is
distributed independently of $c$ --- exchangeability is bought by the
normalisation, not by the split --- and \cref{sec:limitations} returns to what
happens when the model is wrong. The gain over binning is that the whole held-out
half backs every context value, rather than one bin's worth backing each. We implement Mondrian
calibration as well, since it is exactly group-conditional and preferable when a
single context variable dominates; \cref{sec:shift} shows where each wins.

Because a regression can be extrapolated but a guarantee cannot, frames whose
context falls outside the region $\cD$ covers --- a per-coordinate box widened by
a margin, intersected with a Mahalanobis check for jointly novel combinations ---
are reported inactive, and the detector bets neutrally on them. A regime that was
never calibrated cannot be certified, and the abstention rate reports how often
that happens rather than hiding it.

\paragraph{Calibration-conditional validity.}
The same finite $\cD$ scores every frame for months, so the relevant statement is
conditional on $\cD$, and conditionally the p-values deviate from uniform by
$O(n^{-1/2})$. The standard remedy adds the Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz
deviation $\varepsilon_n(\delta) = \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2n)}$
\citep{dkw1956,massart1990} to every p-value. It works and it is far too blunt:
at $n=\CalN{}$, $\delta=\CalDelta{}$ it moves the smallest attainable p-value
from $\CalRawPmin{}$ to $\DkwPmin{}$, capping the evidence a single frame can
supply at well under one nat. The tail is the only part a betting detector
uses.

We use the exact distribution instead.

\begin{theorem}[Exact calibration-conditional levels]
\label{thm:beta}
Let $S_1,\dots,S_n$ be i.i.d.\ from a continuous $F$ and let $S$ be an
independent draw from $F$. For $j = 0,\dots,n-1$ let $\delta_j > 0$ with
$\sum_j \delta_j \le \delta$, and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:betalevels}
  q_j \;=\; \mathrm{Beta}^{-1}\bigl(1-\delta_j;\; j+1,\; n-j\bigr),
  \qquad q_n = 1 .
\end{equation}
Define $\tilde p = q_{J}$ with $J = |\{i : S_i \ge S\}|$. Then there is an event
$E$ with $\mathbb{P}(E) \ge 1-\delta$, measurable with respect to
$S_1,\dots,S_n$, on which
$\mathbb{P}\bigl(\tilde p \le u \mid S_1,\dots,S_n\bigr) \le u$
for every $u \in [0,1]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conditionally on the calibration sample, $\{J \le j\} = \{S > S_{(n-j)}\}$ where
$S_{(1)} \le \cdots \le S_{(n)}$ are the order statistics, so
$\mathbb{P}(J \le j \mid \cD) = 1 - F(S_{(n-j)})$. Since $F$ is continuous,
$F(S_{(k)}) \sim \mathrm{Beta}(k, n+1-k)$, hence
$1 - F(S_{(n-j)}) \sim \mathrm{Beta}(j+1, n-j)$. By \cref{eq:betalevels},
$\mathbb{P}\bigl(1 - F(S_{(n-j)}) > q_j\bigr) = \delta_j$. Let $E$ be the
intersection over $j$ of the complements; a union bound gives
$\mathbb{P}(E) \ge 1 - \sum_j \delta_j \ge 1-\delta$. On $E$,
$\mathbb{P}(\tilde p \le q_j \mid \cD) = \mathbb{P}(J \le j \mid \cD) \le q_j$
for every $j$, and since $\tilde p$ takes values only in $\{q_0,\dots,q_n\}$
and $q_j$ is non-decreasing in $j$, super-uniformity at the atoms extends to all
$u$.
\end{proof}

We spend the budget as $\delta_j \propto (j+1)^{-2}$, concentrating confidence
where the betting functions look; under this allocation $\{q_j\}$ is verified
non-decreasing in $j$, which is what identifies $\{\tilde p \le q_j\} = \{J \le
j\}$ in the proof above (the implementation takes a running maximum as a
safeguard against an allocation for which this fails). At $n=\CalN{}$,
$\delta=\CalDelta{}$ this gives $q_0 = \BetaPmin{}$ against DKW's $\DkwPmin{}$.
\Cref{thm:beta} assumes i.i.d.\ calibration draws, which is why the calibration
set is thinned; \cref{sec:ablation} does not resolve whether skipping the
thinning costs validity at the replication count run there (\cref{sec:limitations}),
though the calibration-conditional argument no longer applies once consecutive
draws are dependent.

Finally, when deployment reweights context that $\cD$ does cover, weighted
conformal calibration \citep{tibshirani2019covariate} applies. We implement it
with a logistic density-ratio estimate, gate it behind a held-out
classifier two-sample test so it engages only when a shift is detectable, and
take the calibration-conditional correction at the Kish effective sample size
\citep{kish1965} in place of $n$ in \cref{eq:betalevels}. Unlike \cref{thm:beta}
itself, this substitution is not accompanied by a proof: \cref{thm:beta}'s
order-statistic argument assumes equal weights, and the Kish size is an
approximation to the effective count under unequal weights that is exact only
in that equal-weight limit, not a derived calibration-conditional bound for the
weighted case. \Cref{sec:shift} reports that in our setting the residual
construction of \cref{eq:residual} dominates it on power, which we take to be a
fact about the setting rather than about the technique: reweighting corrects
the marginal context distribution, while a sequential test is broken by
\emph{conditional} miscalibration; \cref{sec:limitations} returns to what the
weighted variant's validity evidence does and does not show.

\subsection{Layer 2: the episodic e-process}
\label{sec:episodic}

This is the core of the method. Let $p_1, p_2, \ldots$ be the p-values at the
betting instants. A \emph{betting function} is a non-increasing
$f : (0,1] \to [0,\infty)$ with $\int_0^1 f \le 1$; then
$\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[f(p)] \le 1$ for any super-uniform $p$, so $f(p)$ is an
e-value. We use the power family $f_\kappa(p) = \kappa p^{\kappa-1}$,
$\kappa \in (0,1]$, with $\kappa = 1$ the neutral bet $f \equiv 1$.

\subsubsection{Mixing over episodes}

Let $z_t \in \{\text{quiet}, \text{active}\}$ be a two-state Markov chain with
onset probability $\mathbb{P}(\text{quiet} \to \text{active}) = \rho$ and
\emph{end} probability $\mathbb{P}(\text{active} \to \text{quiet}) = \eta$,
started quiet, and let the wealth mix over every trajectory of that chain:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:episodic}
  W_t \;=\; \sum_{z_{1:t}} \mathbb{P}(z_{1:t})
            \prod_{s \le t} f(p_s)^{\ind{z_s = \text{active}}} .
\end{equation}
Each trajectory contributes the product of betting factors over the frames it
declares active, weighted by its prior probability. The classical mixture over
changepoints is the special case in which the chain, once active, can never
return: it mixes over \emph{when} a change starts, whereas \cref{eq:episodic}
mixes over \emph{when it starts and when it stops}, and over how many times that
happens.

The sum has $2^t$ terms and collapses to two numbers. Writing $A_t$ and $Q_t$
for the parts of the sum whose trajectories end active and quiet respectively,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forward}
  A_t = \bigl[(1-\eta)A_{t-1} + \rho\,Q_{t-1}\bigr] f(p_t),
  \qquad
  Q_t = \eta\,A_{t-1} + (1-\rho)\,Q_{t-1},
\end{equation}
with $A_0 = 0$, $Q_0 = 1$ and $W_t = A_t + Q_t$. This is the forward recursion
of a hidden Markov model, and it is $O(1)$ per frame in both time and memory.

\begin{theorem}[Time-uniform validity of the episodic e-process]
\label{thm:episodic}
Under $\Pinf$, if the $p_t$ are conditionally super-uniform given
$\Fcal_{t-1}$, then $(W_t)$ defined by \cref{eq:episodic} is a non-negative
supermartingale with $W_0 = 1$, and for any $\alpha \in (0,1)$
\begin{equation*}
  \Pinf\bigl(\exists\, t \ge 1 : W_t \ge 1/\alpha\bigr) \;\le\; \alpha .
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Non-negativity is immediate and $W_0 = Q_0 = 1$. From \cref{eq:forward},
$A_{t}$ carries exactly one factor $f(p_t)$ and $Q_t$ carries none, so
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}[W_t \mid \Fcal_{t-1}]
   &= \bigl[(1-\eta)A_{t-1} + \rho Q_{t-1}\bigr]\,
      \mathbb{E}[f(p_t) \mid \Fcal_{t-1}]
      \;+\; \eta A_{t-1} + (1-\rho) Q_{t-1} \\
   &\le (1-\eta)A_{t-1} + \rho Q_{t-1} + \eta A_{t-1} + (1-\rho) Q_{t-1}
    \;=\; A_{t-1} + Q_{t-1} \;=\; W_{t-1},
\end{align*}
using $\mathbb{E}[f(p_t) \mid \Fcal_{t-1}] \le 1$ and the fact that the
transition probabilities out of each state sum to one. Ville's inequality
\citep{ville1939} applied to the non-negative supermartingale $(W_t)$ started at
one gives the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[End-to-end level]
\label{cor:composition}
Let $E$ be the calibration event of \cref{thm:beta}, with
$\mathbb{P}(E) \ge 1 - \delta$, on which the deployment p-values $\tilde p_t$
are conditionally super-uniform given $\Fcal_{t-1}$. Then the episodic
e-process run on $(\tilde p_t)$ satisfies
$\Pinf(\exists\, t \ge 1 : W_t \ge 1/\alpha) \le \alpha + \delta$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On $E$, \cref{thm:episodic} applies verbatim and gives probability at most
$\alpha$ conditional on $\cD$; off $E$, the probability is bounded by $1$, and
$\mathbb{P}(E^c) \le \delta$. Removing the conditioning,
$\Pinf(\exists t : W_t \ge 1/\alpha) \le \mathbb{P}(E)\cdot\alpha +
\mathbb{P}(E^c)\cdot 1 \le \alpha + \delta$.
\end{proof}
This is the guarantee actually delivered end to end, and it is what the library
documents (\texttt{sentinel\_e/pipeline.py}) even where earlier drafts of this
text quoted $\alpha$ alone. With the default $\delta = 10^{-3}$ this is a
$10$--$20\%$ relative loosening at the operating points \cref{sec:validity}
reports ($\alpha \in \{0.005,\dots,0.5\}$), not a rounding matter, and every
level plotted in this paper should be read as $\alpha + \delta$.

Two things are worth drawing out. First, the episode structure is \emph{free}:
it needed no new machinery and cost nothing in the guarantee, because the
transition probabilities telescope exactly. Second, the recursion never grows. Evaluated
term by term, a mixture over candidate starts carries one term per start, at
$O(t)$ work per observation \citep{saha2026nonpartitioned}; \cref{eq:forward}
carries two numbers per grid point whatever $t$ is. The geometric-prior
changepoint mixture also telescopes to $O(1)$ (\cref{prop:generalisation}), so
the honest statement is not that we are cheaper than that special case but that
we match it while spanning episodes of finite duration --- month-long monitoring
on edge hardware stays arithmetically trivial (\cref{sec:compute}).

\begin{proposition}[Strict generalisation]
\label{prop:generalisation}
With $\eta = 0$ the recursion \cref{eq:forward} coincides with the mixture over
changepoint locations under a geometric prior with hazard $\rho$; discarding
$Q_t$ from that recovers in turn the classical Shiryaev--Roberts statistic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $\eta = 0$, $Q_t = (1-\rho)Q_{t-1}$ so $Q_t = (1-\rho)^t$ is the prior mass
on ``no episode has begun by $t$'', and
$A_t = (A_{t-1} + \rho Q_{t-1}) f(p_t)$ with $\rho Q_{t-1} = w_t$ the geometric
prior mass on a change at $t$. Unrolling gives
$A_t = \sum_{j \le t} w_j \prod_{s=j}^{t} f(p_s)$, whence
$W_t = \sum_{j\le t} w_j \prod_{s=j}^t f(p_s) + \sum_{j>t} w_j$, Shiryaev's
Bayesian statistic under the geometric prior with hazard $\rho$
\citep{shiryaev1963}. Dropping $Q_t$ leaves $\sum_{j\le t} w_j \prod_{s=j}^t
f(p_s)$ alone, which is not a supermartingale --- the telescoping in
\cref{thm:episodic} fails by exactly $w_t$ per step --- and, taking the
$\rho \to 0$ limit under the appropriate rescaling, recovers the classical
Shiryaev--Roberts sum with unit start weights \citep{roberts1966}; at fixed
$\rho > 0$ the two named statistics remain distinct objects, and only the
proof's algebraic point --- that discarding the quiet mass breaks the
martingale property --- depends on neither.
\end{proof}

So the episodic process sits strictly above both, and the axis it adds ---
episode duration --- is one this anytime-valid e-process family has held
fixed at infinity; \cref{sec:related} positions that claim against the
classical transient- and intermittent-change literature, which has not. The
empirical consequence is in \cref{sec:episodes}: where the classical model is
right, the two coincide within the noise of a paired comparison; where events
recur, a gap opens whose size that same section resolves with uncertainty
rather than by a single point estimate.

\subsubsection{Intermittency, by dilation --- a negative result}
\label{sec:dilation}

Within an episode only a fraction $\pi$ of frames show the act, which suggests
\emph{dilating} the emission:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dilated}
  f_\pi(p) \;=\; (1-\pi) + \pi\, f(p).
\end{equation}
This is legal, and cheaply so: $f_\pi$ is a convex combination of the neutral bet
and $f$, hence non-increasing with $\int_0^1 f_\pi \le 1$, so it is a betting
function and \cref{thm:episodic} applies verbatim. It also says the right thing
--- \emph{this frame belongs to an episode, but it may or may not show the act}.

We expected this to be the answer to intermittency. It is not, and we report
that rather than quietly shipping it. Mixing over $\kappa$ already handles
intermittency: a stream in which a third of the episode frames are anomalous is
best played with a milder bet, and the $\kappa$ grid contains one. A $\pi$ axis
therefore buys no behaviour the grid did not already have, while spending prior
mass to do it. In the ablation, enabling it lengthens censored detection delay
from \AblFullDelay\,s to \AblDilationDelay\,s (\cref{tab:ablation}), and we
found no intermittency level at which it repaid that cost --- below
$\pi \approx 0.2$ both variants fail together, and above it the plain $\kappa$
mixture is ahead. The default grid therefore sets $\pi = 1$.

We keep \cref{eq:dilated} in the exposition and the implementation for two
reasons. It is the natural thing to try, so a reader is owed the evidence rather
than left to repeat the experiment; and the construction remains available for a
backbone whose anomalous frames are far stronger relative to its quiet ones than
ours, where the trade-off could plausibly reverse. What it is not is a
contribution, and we do not count it as one.

\subsubsection{Staying parameter-free}
\label{sec:parameterfree}

Nothing above requires knowing $(\rho, \eta, \pi, \kappa)$. We place a prior grid
over all four and run one recursion per grid point, mixing the results:
$W_t = \sum_g \lambda_g W_t^{(g)}$ with $\sum_g \lambda_g = 1$ is a convex
combination of supermartingales and hence a supermartingale. The deployed grid
is \EpiGridKappa{} values of $\kappa$ by \EpiGridEta{} of $\eta$ by
\EpiGridPi{} of $\pi$ (dilation off by default, \cref{sec:dilation}), i.e.\
\EpiGridPoints{} points, so the per-frame cost is a few dozen floating-point
operations and still $O(1)$ in $t$; enabling the $\pi$ grid (\cref{sec:ablation})
triples the point count and the cost with it, still $O(1)$. In practice the
mixture is what makes the method usable: an operator does not know how long a
dumping event lasts at their site, and the grid spans everything from a
ten-second burst to a permanent change.

\subsubsection{The episode posterior}

The recursion yields, at no extra cost, the posterior probability that the
camera is inside an episode right now,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:posterior}
  \Pi_t \;=\; \frac{A_t}{A_t + Q_t} \;\in\; [0, 1],
\end{equation}
prior-averaged over the grid. This is a more useful thing to look at, and to
transmit, than accumulated wealth: it is bounded, it is interpretable as a
probability, and it is comparable between a camera installed last week and one
that has been running for a month. \Cref{sec:layer3} uses it as the message the
camera graph exchanges.

\subsubsection{Numerics}

Carrying \cref{eq:forward} in log space would need two \texttt{logaddexp} calls
per grid point per frame. Instead we renormalise $(A_t, Q_t)$ to sum to one at
every step and accumulate the logarithm of the scale factor, which is the
standard HMM scaling trick: the two-state simplex cannot underflow, $\log W_t$
is the accumulated log-scale, and the per-step work is a handful of vector
operations on the grid. \Cref{alg:episodic} states the per-frame update in
full; it is a direct transcription of \texttt{sentinel\_e/episodic.py}'s inner
loop, not a simplification of it.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One frame of the episodic e-process, vectorised over the prior grid}
\label{alg:episodic}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require prior grid $\{(\kappa_g, \eta_g, \pi_g)\}_{g=1}^G$ with log-prior
  weights $\log\lambda_g$, $\sum_g \lambda_g = 1$; onset hazard $\rho$ (or
  $\rho_t$, if predictable modulation is enabled, \cref{thm:predictable});
  alarm threshold $1/\alpha$
\State $A_g \gets 0,\; Q_g \gets 1,\; s_g \gets 0 \;\; \forall g$
  \Comment{$s_g$: per-grid-point log-scale; $A_g + Q_g = 1$ is the invariant}
\For{each betting instant $t = 1, 2, \ldots$}
  \State receive conformal p-value $p_t$ (or skip this $t$: thinning / abstention)
  \For{each grid point $g$ \textbf{(vectorised, not a real loop)}}
    \State $f_g \gets (1 - \pi_g) + \pi_g\, \kappa_g\, p_t^{\,\kappa_g - 1}$
      \Comment{dilated power bet, \cref{eq:dilated}; $\pi_g = 1$ if dilation off}
    \State $A_g' \gets \bigl[(1-\eta_g)\, A_g + \rho\, Q_g\bigr] f_g$
    \State $Q_g' \gets \eta_g\, A_g + (1-\rho)\, Q_g$
      \Comment{\cref{eq:forward}, one grid point}
    \State $c_g \gets A_g' + Q_g'$
      \Comment{restores the invariant; $c_g > 0$ always}
    \State $A_g \gets A_g' / c_g,\;\; Q_g \gets Q_g' / c_g$
      \Comment{HMM scaling trick: the two-state simplex cannot underflow}
    \State $s_g \gets s_g + \log c_g$
  \EndFor
  \State $\log W_t \gets \operatorname{logsumexp}_g\bigl(\log\lambda_g + s_g\bigr)$
    \Comment{mixture happens here, in log space, not by renormalising a joint sum}
  \State $\Pi_t \gets \sum_g \lambda_g\, e^{s_g} A_g \,\big/\, e^{\log W_t}$
    \Comment{episode posterior, \cref{eq:posterior}}
  \If{$\log W_t \ge \log(1/\alpha)$}
    \State \textbf{alarm}; if restarting, reset $A_g \gets 0,\, Q_g \gets 1,\,
      s_g \gets 0\;\; \forall g$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Two things about \cref{alg:episodic} are worth making explicit because they are
easy to miss on a first read of \cref{eq:forward} alone. First, every grid
point keeps its \emph{own} running invariant $A_g + Q_g = 1$ and its own
log-scale $s_g$; the mixture over the grid is not a joint renormalisation but a
single \texttt{logsumexp} taken only when $\log W_t$ or $\Pi_t$ is read out, so
adding grid points costs a longer vector, not a different algorithm. Second,
the inner loop over $g$ is the only place the grid appears and it is fully
vectorised in the implementation (one array operation per line, not a Python
loop), so nothing about the recursion's cost depends on how the grid was
chosen: the parameter-free stance of \cref{sec:parameterfree} costs exactly
$|\{g\}|$ times the single-hypothesis cost, not more. The normalising division
(line 8) is exact, not a truncation or a clip, so no approximation enters
between \cref{eq:forward} and what the code computes beyond ordinary
floating-point rounding, which \cref{sec:validity}'s oracle-p-value arm is
designed to expose if it mattered.

Three things this trace makes concrete that the general statement can leave
abstract. First, a single small p-value ($t=1$, $p_1=0.02$, a moderately
surprising frame) moves $A_t$ from $0$ to $0.045$ in one step but moves $W_t$
by less than $4\%$ --- the betting factor $f_1 = 4.64$ is diluted by the prior
mass $Q_0 = 1$ still sitting on ``no episode yet'', which is the entire content
of mixing over onset times rather than betting on frame one being the change.
Second, $W_t$ genuinely falls below $1$ at $t=3$ ($p_3 = 0.9$, an unsurprising
frame under the null): this is the supermartingale property in miniature ---
wealth is not required to grow, only to have conditional expectation at most
its previous value --- and it is why a single early ``lucky'' small p-value
cannot, by itself, manufacture a violation of \cref{thm:episodic}. Third,
$A_t \ll Q_t$ throughout this short trace, because four frames is not enough
evidence at this $\kappa$ to move the posterior far from ``quiet'' even though
one of the four p-values was small; \cref{eq:posterior}'s $\Pi_t = A_t$ here
(the single-grid-point case), so this trace's $\Pi_4 \approx 0.049$ is a small
number for an honest reason, not a sign that anything failed.

\subsection{Layer 3: the camera network}
\label{sec:layer3}

\paragraph{A predictable spatial prior.}
Offenders return to the same lay-by and, when disturbed, move a few hundred
metres and dump again, so an episode at one camera is genuine prior information
about its neighbours. We build a graph over the fleet with edge weights
combining spatial proximity and static-context similarity, and a two-layer
message-passing network maps each camera's features to an onset hazard
$\rho_{i,t}$ and a stake scale $m_{i,t}$.

The features are where the episode posterior of \cref{eq:posterior} earns its
place. A camera's log-wealth grows without bound and depends on how long it has
been running, so aggregating raw wealth over a neighbourhood lets one
long-installed camera dominate its neighbours regardless of what is happening
there. $\Pi_{i,t}$ is bounded in $[0,1]$ and answers a question that means the
same thing at every camera --- \emph{is there an episode here now?} --- so a mean
over neighbours is meaningful. The network sees $\Pi_{i,t-1}$, the normalised
log-wealth, three exponential moving averages of $-\log p$, static context and
degree, all measurable at $t-1$.

Because the network passes messages, camera $i$'s inputs at $t$ --- and hence
$\rho_{i,t}$ and $m_{i,t}$ --- depend on neighbours' features and so on the
\emph{fleet's} joint history, not camera $i$'s own. Write $\Gfilt_{t-1} =
\sigma(p_{j,s} : j \in \text{fleet},\, s \le t-1)$ for that joint filtration;
$\Fcal_{t-1} \subseteq \Gfilt_{t-1}$ is camera $i$'s own. The theorem below is
stated, and needed, at the joint level.

\begin{theorem}[Learned control cannot break the guarantee]
\label{thm:predictable}
Suppose $\rho_{i,t} \in [0,1]$ and $m_{i,t} \in [0,1]$ are
$\Gfilt_{t-1}$-measurable, the map $(m,\kappa) \mapsto f_{\pi,\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}$
with $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1-m(1-\kappa)$ is jointly measurable, and $p_{i,t}$
is conditionally super-uniform given $\Gfilt_{t-1}$ (the joint history, not
merely camera $i$'s own). Then \cref{thm:episodic} holds verbatim for every
camera, for any such choice of $\rho_{i,t}, m_{i,t}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $m_{i,t} \in [0,1]$ and $\kappa \in (0,1]$ we have
$\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}} \in (0,1]$, so $f_{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ is a betting
function and by \cref{eq:dilated} so is its dilation, for every fixed value of
$\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Since $(m_{i,t}, \pi)$ is $\Gfilt_{t-1}$-measurable and
the map to $f_{\pi,\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ is jointly measurable in its
argument and in $p_{i,t}$, conditioning on $\Gfilt_{t-1}$ freezes
$\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$ to its realised value and
$\mathbb{E}[f_{\pi,\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}(p_{i,t}) \mid \Gfilt_{t-1}] \le 1$
follows from conditional super-uniformity at that fixed value --- this is the
standard freezing argument for a regular conditional distribution, not a
step that treats $f_{\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ as linear in $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
Replacing $\rho$ by $\rho_{i,t}$ in \cref{eq:forward} leaves the transition
probabilities out of each state summing to one, which is all the telescoping in
\cref{thm:episodic} uses, now run against $\Gfilt_{t-1}$ throughout. The
supermartingale step is therefore unchanged.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis that matters in practice is the conditional super-uniformity of
$p_{i,t}$ given the \emph{joint} fleet history, which is strictly stronger than
what \cref{sec:layer1} establishes for a single camera in isolation: the
message-passing layer is built to couple cameras that share weather, lighting
and traffic, which is exactly the kind of cross-camera dependence that could in
principle carry information about $p_{i,t}$ beyond camera $i$'s own past.
\Cref{sec:diagnostics} does not certify this stronger condition; it is assumed
here, and the honest scope of \cref{thm:predictable} is conditional on it
holding, with e-BH's arbitrary-dependence guarantee at the combination step
providing no help at the level of a single camera's e-value. What the theorem
still delivers unconditionally is narrower but real: whatever
$\rho_{i,t}, m_{i,t}$ the network outputs, it enters only through a predictable
multiplicative reweighting of a supermartingale step that already holds or
already fails on its own terms, so a badly-behaved learned component cannot
be the \emph{cause} of an invalid step that a well-behaved one would not also
have. \Cref{tab:network}'s all-null columns check the practical consequence
directly, since a theorem about the idealised object is not by itself evidence
about the implementation.

Training is offline on held-out simulated fleets, maximising post-change
log-wealth growth --- the Kelly / GROW criterion \citep{kelly1956}, the standard
proxy for minimising delay, since the delay is essentially the time for the
wealth to climb from one to $1/\alpha$ at that rate --- with a penalty on wealth
burned at unaffected cameras. The recursion is differentiable, so gradients
reach both heads.

\paragraph{Fleet decisions.}
At a stopping time $\tau_i = \min(\text{alarm}, T)$, optional stopping
gives $\Einf[W_{i,\tau_i}] \le 1$, so each camera's wealth is a valid e-value.
We apply e-BH \citep{wang2022ebh}: sort $E_{(1)} \ge \cdots \ge E_{(K)}$ and
reject the top $k^\star = \max\{k : E_{(k)} \ge K/(\alpha k)\}$. This controls
FDR at $\alpha$ under \emph{arbitrary} dependence, which is what the setting
demands: adjacent cameras share weather, lighting and traffic, and Layer 3
couples them deliberately. BH on p-values would need positive regression
dependence or a Benjamini--Yekutieli $\log K$ penalty
\citep{benjamini2001}; e-BH needs neither. A running maximum of the wealth
would not be a valid e-value, which is why the reading is taken at a stopping
time.
