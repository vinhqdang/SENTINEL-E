\section{Unpaired episode comparisons (descriptive)}
\label{sec:esm-episodes}

\Cref{fig:esm-episodes}, \cref{tab:episodes} and \cref{tab:intermittency}
compare the episodic and changepoint mixtures
without pairing the seeds or matching the grids. The episodic arm uses its
$6\times4$ grid, and the changepoint arm uses 32 log-spaced values of $\kappa$.
Each row uses independent seeds. These tables are descriptive. The claims in
the main text rest on the paired comparison (Sect.~4.5), which removes the grid
confound and uses shared seeds.

The caption of \Cref{tab:episodes} states that the two mixtures agree with
one episode. The paired comparison does not support that reading. At one episode
the episodic mixture is behind by \EpiPairedDiffOne\,s (95\% CI
\EpiPairedCIOne{}), a difference that the main text reports as resolved.

The stream miss rates in \Cref{tab:episodes} for two episodes differ from
those in the main-text per-episode table. Those are independent runs of the same
configuration, and differences of this size at 200 streams per row are within
Monte Carlo error.

\section{Shorter horizon sweep}
\label{sec:esm-horizon}

\Cref{tab:horizon} sweeps the horizon from about 10 minutes to 2.7\,h at
$\alpha=0.01$, with 200 streams per point. SENTINEL-E keeps a false-alarm
probability of zero. The per-frame rules approach one, and the p-value threshold reaches one by 40 minutes. The
week-long sweep in the main text, Sect.~4.4, extends this to 168\,h with a
realistic day.

\section{Simulator configuration}
\label{sec:esm-simulator}

The simulator generates one scalar per frame. Its parameters are the defaults of
\texttt{sentinel\_e.streams.StreamConfig}, and the experiments override only
those listed here.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diurnal period.} \SimDiurnalFrames{} frames per simulated day,
  which is \SimDiurnalMin{}\,min at 25\,fps. Experiments 1--12 use this period.
  The week-long experiment uses a 24\,h day of $2{,}160{,}000$ frames instead.
  \item \textbf{Calibration.} \SimCalN{} raw no-dumping frames per camera by
  default, which is \HorizonWeekCalDefaultPct\% of a day. The week-long
  experiment instead calibrates on one full day of \HorizonWeekCalN{} frames.
  \item \textbf{Weather and activity.} A two-state weather chain shifts the null
  score. A log-scale Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process drives the scene activity. It
  is not part of the context the conformal layer bins on.
  \item \textbf{Score noise.} AR(1) innovations with Student-$t$ tails, so the
  per-frame scores are autocorrelated and heavy-tailed.
  \item \textbf{Episodes.} Episodes are separated by exponential gaps, with a
  fraction $\pi$ of frames anomalous inside each episode and a ramp at each end.
  \item \textbf{Fleet propagation.} An episode propagates to a neighbour with
  probability \NetPropProb{} and a mean lag of \NetPropLagSec{}\,s.
\end{itemize}
