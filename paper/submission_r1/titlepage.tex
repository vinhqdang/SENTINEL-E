\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Visual-anomaly detectors, including illegal-dumping detectors, are evaluated per clip but deployed on continuous streams, where alerting at the first threshold crossing is optional stopping. Fed valid 1\% p-values, a per-frame rule raises a false alarm on \ValidityPfaPvaluePct\% of 40-minute null streams. We introduce the episodic e-process, a mixture over episodes that start, flicker, end and recur. It is a non-negative supermartingale when the p-values are conditionally super-uniform, so Ville's inequality gives time-uniform false-alarm control; it contains the changepoint mixture as the case where episodes never end. A two-state recursion evaluates it in constant time per frame. In simulation it raised no false alarm on 200 week-long streams, where per-frame thresholds reach 0.980 within an hour. Paired comparisons show episodes shorten delay for long, intermittent, recurring events; the changepoint mixture detects a single episode faster, and a permanent change with equivalent delay. On real video (Ped2, Avenue, Mivia-IWDD), operating points come from training videos, and each test video is monitored alone. Thinned p-values remain serially dependent, so real-video validity is verified empirically. Per-video false-alarm proportions are consistent with the nominal level at $\alpha=0.1$ on all three corpora (3, 16 and 50 null videos). On Mivia-IWDD, PatchCore raises alarms on 9 of 50 normal videos (95\% interval [0.086, 0.314]) and detects 10 of 50 dumping events. Detection power depends on the backbone. On these short videos, which do not exercise the horizon-free guarantee, several baselines detect more events with more false alarms. SENTINEL-E costs under two microseconds per frame.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.5em}

\section*{Highlights}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An episodic e-process mixes over events that start, end and recur
  \item Time-uniform false-alarm control at constant cost per frame
  \item No false alarm in 200 simulated week-long streams; per-frame rules reach 0.980 in 1 h
  \item Leak-free per-video evaluation on Ped2, Avenue and Mivia-IWDD dumping videos
  \item On real video, power depends on the backbone; validity is verified empirically
\end{itemize}
