## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor

dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: For the formal core — whether each theorem/proposition states its assumptions explicitly, whether every assumption is actually used and is sufficient (filtration and adaptedness specified, optional-stopping/maximal-inequality invocations matched to the martingale or supermartingale property actually established, sub-e-process versus e-process distinctions kept straight, time-uniform versus fixed-time coverage not conflated, and any exchangeability requirement for conformal components stated); for the Monte-Carlo protocol — number of independent replications, seeding and RNG discipline, the data-generating process written down in enough detail to regenerate it, Monte-Carlo standard errors or confidence intervals on every headline number (especially empirical Type-I error / false-alarm rate against the nominal level, and detection-delay quantiles), whether operating points and hyperparameters were selected on the same runs used to report performance, whether baselines were tuned with comparable effort, and whether the simulation regimes span the assumption boundaries the theory relies on rather than only the favourable interior; for reproducibility — whether the released artefacts (results JSON, tables, figures, library tests, reproduction scripts) map one-to-one onto the reported quantities with an executable path from script to table cell, and whether declared software/hardware and runtime make that path feasible.
what_triggers_block: A defect that invalidates a reported quantity or a stated guarantee as currently written but is repairable within the existing design — for example a proof step that is materially incomplete or relies on an unstated assumption that is plausibly true and could be added (a missing integrability or measurability condition, an unjustified interchange of limit and expectation, a supermartingale property asserted rather than verified); headline error-control or delay numbers reported as point estimates with no Monte-Carlo uncertainty or with a replication count too small to distinguish the claimed effect from noise; thresholds, calibration sets or hyperparameters selected on the same simulation runs that report the performance, with no held-out or pre-registered split; baselines run at default settings while the proposed method is tuned; or artefacts that do not reconstruct the reported tables (missing seeds, undeclared configuration, a stated number with no traceable source in the released results).
what_triggers_warn: A rigour shortfall that weakens confidence in, but does not invalidate, the reported quantities and can be closed by adding material rather than redoing work — for example assumptions stated correctly but only in prose and never restated where invoked, a proof that is correct yet compresses a step enough that verification requires reconstruction, Monte-Carlo error reported for some headline numbers but not others, a replication budget adequate for the mean but thin for the tail quantiles being emphasised, simulation regimes that omit an obvious stress case (assumption-violating dependence, distribution shift, or adversarial timing) without argument for the omission, sensitivity to a tuning constant left unexplored, or reproduction scripts present and plausible but requiring undocumented manual steps to reach the reported figures.
what_triggers_fatal: A defect that cannot be repaired without withdrawing or fundamentally restating a result — for example a proof containing a genuine logical error (an invalid inequality direction, circular use of the conclusion, optional stopping applied to a process that is not a supermartingale under the stated filtration, or an anytime-valid claim derived from a fixed-time argument) such that the theorem as stated is false or its hypotheses are unsatisfiable by the procedure actually deployed; a counterexample constructible from the paper's own setting; a simulation protocol whose estimand is not the quantity claimed (for instance false-alarm control evaluated only on runs conditioned on a change having occurred, or a detection-delay statistic computed only over detected runs with no accounting for missed ones) so that the reported numbers cannot bear on the claim regardless of sample size; or reported results that are contradicted by, or absent from, the supplied artefacts in a way indicating the numbers were not produced by the described procedure.

### D3: argumentative_coherence

dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether the guarantee the theory establishes and the guarantee the paper advertises are the same object — tracing each abstract, introduction, and conclusion claim back to the specific theorem, proposition, or experiment that discharges it, and checking that the conditions attached upstream survive the trip; whether the class of claim matches the class of evidence, i.e. universal or worst-case assertions rest on proof rather than on simulation, while simulation is used for the properties it can legitimately establish (existence of favourable regimes, comparative behaviour under stated generative conditions, sensitivity, illustration of a proved rate); whether the boundary between what is proved and what is observed is drawn explicitly and held consistently throughout, rather than blurred by the time the discussion section arrives; whether deployment or operational claims for a real surveillance setting are presented as extrapolations from synthetic data rather than as demonstrated; and whether the stated limitations actually name the binding constraints (simulation-only evidence, assumption dependence, absence of real-stream validation) instead of substituting minor caveats.
what_triggers_block: A load-bearing claim whose supporting evidence is of the wrong class or narrower than the claim — a general or unconditional statement about error control, robustness, or detection performance supported only by simulation under the paper's own generative model; a theorem's conditional guarantee restated without its conditions where the omission changes what a reader would conclude; performance advantages over baselines asserted as general when they hold only in the simulated regimes shown, with no scope qualifier; operational readiness or field efficacy for the surveillance application asserted on synthetic evidence alone; or an internal inconsistency in which two parts of the paper commit to incompatible characterisations of what has been established.
what_triggers_warn: Overreach that is local and correctable by qualification without disturbing the argument's structure — an abstract or conclusion sentence stronger than the body it summarises, a comparative claim missing the regime qualifier that appears correctly elsewhere, an implication for practice drawn a step further than the evidence reaches but not relied upon downstream, or a limitations section that acknowledges the simulation-only design but does not connect it to the specific claims it constrains.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis itself is not established by the paper's own argument — the headline contribution depends on a step that the results do not supply and cannot supply as designed (an anytime-valid or uniform-over-time claim resting on evidence that is only pointwise or only empirical, or a claim of validity under conditions the procedure demonstrably violates); the paper's formal results and its empirical findings assert mutually contradictory things about the method's behaviour with no reconciliation; or the argument is circular at its core, with an assumption doing the work the conclusion is meant to do.
